# Emberwake: world and story

An original high-fantasy world in the spirit of the big party-and-war fantasies: a grim, morally grey frontier, a continent-scale war, and a company of misfits with secrets. Every name, place, character and event here is new; nothing is borrowed from an existing game, book or film.

**Logline.** A mountain range is the corpse of a dragon, and everyone has built a life on her bones. When a grieving necromancer sings the dead awake to settle a debt with the crown, a burnt-out knight, a dwarf in a war-mech, an elf with a nine-hundred-year-old secret and a squirrel assassin have one night to keep the mountain asleep. Then the mountain opens its eye.

**Tagline.** *The mountain remembers.*

## The world: the Spine of Vharra

A thousand years ago the Ember-Mother, **Vharra**, the last of the elder dragons, lay down across the north of the continent and did not get up. Snow covered her. Forests grew in her shadow. Over the centuries, people stopped calling the ridge her spine and started calling it *the Spine*. Most of them no longer know it was ever anything else.

Ribs the size of cathedrals still break through the snow on the high passes. The stone there is warm in winter.

Three peoples live off her body, and one remembers what she is.

| People | Where | What they take from her | What they believe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Humans of the **Kingdom of Calder** | **Lanternhold**, a walled city at the mountain's feet | Warmth, safe valleys, and *ember*, the glowing dust in her old wounds, which burns in their lanterns for a year and keeps the dead from rising | The dragon is a story for children. The lanterns are tradition. |
| Dwarves of **Anvilmouth** | A forge-city carved into what was her jaw | *Emberbone*: her bones still hold heat, and one shard runs a forge, or a war-mech, for a generation | Bone is ore. Ore is meant to be dug. |
| Elves of the **Amberwood** | A forest of golden trees on her flank, roots deep in her hide | Long life: the forest drinks her slow heartbeat | She is asleep, not dead. She must never wake, whatever it costs. |
| The dead of **Hollowmere** | An ash-grey ruin on the frozen lake below the Spine | Nothing. They were taken from. | Nothing yet. They are waiting for someone to sing. |

### Ember and the dead

In this world the dead do not stay down on their own. A body left in the cold for a winter will stand up in the spring. Ember stops it: burning ember keeps a grave quiet. This is why Calder's lanterns burn on every wall, and why the Lantern Watch, Calder's order of knights, burns ember at every crossroads and every grave.

Necromancy is the art of singing to the dead in the voice ember silences. Calder outlawed it three hundred years ago. In Calder, a necromancer is a *cantor*.

### What everyone is hiding

- **The Crown.** Twelve years ago a fever swept Hollowmere. King Osric III feared the dead would rise faster than the Watch could burn them, so he sealed the city's gates and burned it with everyone inside. The official story is "the fever took Hollowmere." Aldric Venn held the gate that night.
- **The dwarves.** Anvilmouth's deepest shaft, the Heartcut, has reached something warm that beats once an hour. The clan council voted to keep digging. Brunna's drill was first through the wall.
- **The elves.** Vharra did not lie down to die. She lay down to sleep, grieving her last hatchling, Kesh, who was lost the same year. Nobody knows what took Kesh. Ithrael knows: the elves killed Kesh, so the mother would fall into a grief deep enough to sleep, and stop burning their forests. Ithrael was the archer.
- **The squirrels.** The **Order of the Quiet Acorn** has worked as an assassins' guild for all four peoples for four hundred years. It belongs to none of them. Its one loyalty is the oldest tree in the Amberwood, which grows from Vharra's heart, and its creed is "the tree first." It has always known what the mountain is.

## Synopsis

Saevra Duskwyn was Hollowmere's healer. She was outside the walls, gathering winter herbs, the night the king burned the city. She came back to ash. She has spent twelve years learning to sing to the dead, and has learned that ember only quiets them; it doesn't put them to rest.

She wants a reckoning, and she has found the instrument for it. In the ice beneath Hollowmere lake lie the bones of a young dragon. She sings them awake: **Kesh**, Vharra's lost child, now a skeleton held together by song. If the child's voice calls, the mother will wake, and the Ember-Mother's first breath will burn Lanternhold and its king off her feet.

Saevra doesn't know who killed Kesh. She assumes it was humans. Everyone does.

On the night of the first snow, the dead of Hollowmere rise in their thousands, the Hollow Choir, and march on Lanternhold with Kesh flying above them. The lanterns go out one by one. Saevra's song does that.

Four unlikely defenders hold the wall:

- **Aldric Venn**, the last captain of the Lantern Watch. He is too old for this and carries the guilt of the Hollowmere gate.
- **Brunna Coalhand**, a dwarf forge-mother who came to Lanternhold to sell war-mechs and stayed to fight in one, **Old Grudge**, a mech with an emberbone heart.
- **Ithrael Sunmourn**, the elven warden sent "to help." Her real orders are to make sure Vharra doesn't wake, at any price, including Lanternhold.
- **Whisk**, a red squirrel of the Order of the Quiet Acorn, hired by someone to kill someone on the wall tonight. She won't say who.

The trailer ends on the moment the story turns. The Spine lifts, a head the size of a castle rises out of the mountain, and the Ember-Mother asks one question: *"Who took my child?"*

Every head on the battlefield, living and dead, turns toward the elf.

## The film beyond the trailer (for tone, not for the render)

- Whisk's contract is on Ithrael. The Order wants Vharra awake: the tree first, and the tree is dying because its heart is asleep.
- Saevra learns that the elves, not the crown, killed Kesh. She has to choose between her revenge and the truth, with a grieving dragon listening.
- Aldric's arc is confession. Brunna's is the Heartcut: she has been drilling into a living mother.
- There is no clean villain. Everyone took something from the mountain.

## Tone

- Grim, grounded and morally grey, like a monster-hunter saga: mud, frost, tired eyes, and nobody is purely good.
- Epic in scale, like a war-of-the-world MMO cinematic: armies, a dragon over a city, mechs marching, the score swelling.
- A party you fall for, like a modern tabletop RPG: banter under pressure, secrets, and one perfect comedy beat in the middle of the carnage.
- Squirrels are deadly and adorable at once. Play them dead straight; the comedy comes from everyone else's reactions.
